# Allocation diagnostic and repeated step stops

The original native v3 capture stopped after 443 verified read pairs because the collector recorded two identical free entries without an intervening return. Its step logic rearmed the consumed breakpoint and interpreted a step stop at that same location as another call. A host regression reproduced the ambiguity; the v3 partial result remains unknown and establishes neither a BIOS double free nor a leak.

The repaired collector keeps the consumed breakpoint disabled until a step leaves that thread/PC/ESP identity, with at most eight attempts. It records returned step replies plus the observed registers and current thread separately from allocation/free events. Changed unrelated registers do not establish a fresh call at the same identity. Exhausted retries, lost reads and failed cleanup retain error/unknown. A genuine subsequent free after a normal return remains a separate event. A returned reply is omitted if its following register/thread read fails, so the control ledger is not a complete wire transcript; that failure still makes the trace unknown.

Historical native v4 completed all 1,024 ordered NtReadFile issue/consumer pairs, validating API status, IOSB, length and full-byte comparison. It observed three T05thread:01; stops with unchanged thread and all nine recorded registers; each advanced on the next step, including one at ExFreePoolWithTag entry. This directly demonstrates the old collector's replay condition. It does not prove the underlying emulator/CPU cause or retroactively supply v3's missing raw reply.

The v4 stream observed 1,025 successful allocations and 1,024 completed frees. Static selected-image symbols place 1,024 872-byte DMA allocations at HalGetScatterGatherList+61 and their frees at HalPutScatterGatherList+83. The remaining 128-byte FatF allocation falls at VfatPrepareSeekCache+91 and remained live at the final consumer boundary before later handle cleanup. That observation is not a leak determination or authenticated storage attribution.

The 1,000-byte simultaneous peak is the observed requested-byte live set between allocator/free return boundaries, not physical charge or BIOS footprint. Boot inventory is incomplete and all owner classifications remain unknown; zero declared-storage counts reflect absent attribution. Worker hops, lower command and bounce-copy counts are unavailable. Raw ServiceCount readings do not qualify IRQ counts. This instrumented window differs from the public batch timer and has no latency/performance acceptance eligibility. Combined P1 and major-driver gates remain open.
